2. Play with actions: preserve the actual illustrated cow eating grass, rabbit eating carrot, walking and sleeping animations, including replay and calm stopping. Model short action words and sentences. Existing standalone sentence and optional alphabet activities remain accessible.

## Proposed first episode
Choose Rabbit → Morning with Rabbit. Three short beats for the first build: wake up and greet a parent; brush teeth together; eat breakfast. One simple action and one spoken model per beat. Example models: “Good morning!”, “Brush your teeth”, “I am eating.” An animated outcome must show the chosen action, not just display a sentence or decorative movement. Model language; never require repetition, microphone recognition or reading. A word, gesture or Hindi response is welcome; parent can expand it naturally.

Use the existing rabbit eating animation as appropriate within pretend play. Do not frame real rabbits eating carrots as a complete diet or human routines as facts about animal biology. A cow variant must retain cow identity and appropriate food. Choose small meaningful variations, not separate worlds with unrelated mechanics.

Later chapters: tidy the bed, wash/get dressed, pack a bag, walk to school, greet friends/teacher, request help/water, take turns, return home, put toys away, bedtime. These are a content roadmap, not one long compulsory session. Save a checkpoint after each beat; resume next time. Existing short shared time/turn limits remain. ## Research and its limits
- ASHA's 3–4-year guidance recommends simple stories, acting out everyday activities and using the languages parents are comfortable speaking: https://www.asha.org/public/developmental-milestones/communication-milestones-3-to-4-years/ . Supports routine-based language play; does not prescribe these exact levels or prove an app effective.
- NAEYC discusses language and vocabulary in meaningful dramatic play: https://www.naeyc.org/resources/pubs/tyc/winter2024/creating-dramatic-play-areas-to-support-literacy . This is educational guidance, not a trial of this game.
- AAP recommends adult co-viewing/co-playing and discussion: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/why-co-viewing-is-important-tips-to-share-screen-time-with-your-kids.aspx . Design for parent participation and off-screen connections.
- Ganea et al. (2014) studied preschoolers learning facts about real animals from picture books: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3989584/ . Fully anthropomorphic presentation sometimes reduced factual transfer. This does NOT establish that our animal-led routine game will fail or succeed at teaching English or habits. It is a reason to distinguish pretend play from animal facts and verify real-world transfer instead of assuming it.

Design inference: familiar routines plus a favourite character are a reasonable experiment for this child. The game can rehearse words and actions; actual habits still require real-life adult-supported practice. Example bridge: after Rabbit brushes, parent connects it to the child's own toothbrush/routine. Avoid chore scores, obedience labels or pressure to perform.
